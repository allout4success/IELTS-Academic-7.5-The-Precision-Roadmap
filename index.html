Lesson 1: Listening Question Architectures & Band 7.5+ Tactics

Every IELTS Listening section follows predictable signpost cues and distractor patterns. Master all 6 formats below.

Practice on IELTSGuru.ai PRO ↗
Type 1 • Parts 1 & 4

Form / Note / Table / Flow-Chart Completion

Strategy: Predict the exact grammatical class (noun, adjective, number, postcode) and word limit (e.g., NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS) during the 30-second preview.
Distractor Trap: Speakers frequently self-correct numbers or spellings ("It's B-R-O-W-N... oh sorry, no E at the end"). Always write the final confirmed version.

Type 2 • Parts 2 & 3

Multiple Choice (Single & Multiple Answer)

Strategy: Underline only the core differentiating keyword in options A, B, and C. Speakers will mention words from all three options.
Signpost Cue: Listen for contrast markers ("However, what actually tipped the balance was...") which signal the true answer after two distractors.

Type 3 • Part 2

Plan / Map / Diagram Labelling

Strategy: Immediately locate the "You are here" entrance or compass rose before audio begins. Trace your finger physically along the route.
Key Prepositions: adjacent to, directly opposite, just past, in the far northwest corner, branching off to the left.

Type 4 • Part 3

Matching Information / Speakers

Strategy: Questions follow the exact chronological order of the audio, while the lettered box (A–G) is paraphrased.
Band 7.5+ Edge: Focus on speaker attitude verbs (skeptical about, convinced by, ambivalent toward) rather than matching identical nouns.

Type 5 • Parts 3 & 4

Sentence Completion

Strategy: Ensure your completed sentence is 100% grammatically sound (check singular/plural subject-verb agreement and articles).
Signpost Cue: Academic lectures use structural markers: "Turning now to...", "The primary catalyst behind this was...".

Type 6 • Parts 1 & 2

Short-Answer Questions

Strategy: Identify the exact WH-question target (Who, What factor, Which facility, How much) and copy exact words heard in the audio.
Check: Never exceed the stated word/number limit; hyphenated words (e.g., check-in) count as one word.

The 15 – 20 – 25 Minute Pacing Rule & Question Strategies

Academic Reading passages increase in lexical density. Allocate 15 mins to Passage 1, 20 mins to Passage 2, and 25 mins to Passage 3.

Practice on IELTSGuru.ai PRO ↗
Type 1 • In Order

True / False / Not Given & Yes / No / Not Given

Falsification Rule: If you can write a sentence that directly contradicts the text, it is FALSE/NO. If the text neither confirms nor contradicts the specific claim (e.g., introduces a comparison not in the text), it is NOT GIVEN.
Qualifier Watch: Watch out for all, solely, predominantly, rarely.

Type 2 • Out of Order

Matching Headings

Strategy: Do this first for a passage. Read the first two and last sentence of Paragraph A to extract the central topic, then match to the heading list.
Trap: Never pick a heading just because it shares an identical word with a minor example inside the paragraph.

Type 3 • Out of Order

Matching Information to Paragraphs

Strategy: Leave this type until after you complete in-order questions (like T/F/NG or Summary) for that passage so you already know where key ideas live.
Clue Words: Look for category nouns in the prompt: "an explanation of...", "a comparison between...", "a reference to...".

Type 4 • Usually In Order

Summary / Note / Table Completion

Strategy: Use the summary title to pinpoint the exact 2–3 paragraphs in the passage. Determine the needed part of speech before scanning.
Word Box Variant: Eliminate options by grammar first, then match semantic synonyms.

Type 5 • In Order

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)

Strategy: Read the question stem (not the 4 options yet), locate the exact sentence in the text, formulate your own mental answer, and only then compare against A–D.
Elimination: Cross out options that are True in general life but unmentioned in the text.

Type 6 • Out of Order / In Order

Matching Features & Sentence Endings

Matching Researchers/Dates: Scan the whole passage and highlight every proper name/year first.
Sentence Endings: Combine syntactic fit (clause structure) with exact textual evidence.

    Connected Speech & Rhythm Mastery

    Native speakers and IELTS Listening recordings compress function words. Master these 4 phenomena:

    • Catenation (Consonant-to-Vowel Linking): "an_aim" sounds like "a name"; "turn_it_off" flows as a single rhythmic unit.
    • Weak Forms & Schwa (/ə/): Prepositions & auxiliaries (to, for, of, have, been) reduce in unstressed positions ("could have been" → /kʊdəv bɪn/).
    • Elision (Dropping /t/ and /d/): "next week" → /neks wiːk/; "most common" → /məʊs kɒmən/.
    • Assimilation: "would you" → /wʊdʒuː/; "in Paris" → /ɪm pærɪs/.
